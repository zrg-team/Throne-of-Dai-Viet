# Icon critique — action and small-size clarity

The previous review overvalued recognition of a simple flag. The user liked the drawing style but said that the subjects did not feel like their game. This pass evaluates the promise of gameplay before selecting a compact silhouette.

## Three initial concepts

| Concept | What works | What fails | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: cavalry charge | Immediately active. The red rider, cream horse and angled spear make an energetic unit silhouette. Light cavalry exists in the game. | The long spear and hooves approach the corners. Rider, harness and dust compete at small sizes. A rider can suggest a hero action game, and the horse remains close to an earlier rejected subject. | Keep as an alternative, not the recommendation. |
| B: formation clash | Two sides and a weapon contact express the game's attack/defense choices. Red attackers and green defenders establish opposition. | Six full-body figures create too many small heads, limbs and shafts. Several figures hit the frame edge. The contact point becomes a small knot at 48 px. | Strongest gameplay relationship; simplify and enlarge the interaction. |
| C: river ambush | The boat, stakes and waves refer to the game's Bạch Đằng-inspired Chronicle event. Its sail makes a distinctive large silhouette. | The sail dominates, making the small icon read as a boat before a trap. The stakes read ambiguously as rocks. This could overpromise a primarily naval game; naval maneuvering is not the core battle loop. | Keep as a historical-story alternative. |

## Refinement one

Reduced the six soldiers to two, enlarged the shield, simplified the garments and focused on one spear impact. This improved the large color shapes. However, ImageGen rendered the circular safe-area instruction as a visible oval frame despite an explicit no-border instruction. The frame consumed room and made the result read as a badge. It was rejected as a final export.

## Refinement two: current candidate

Removed the oval, kept the faces secondary, and allowed the lower garments to continue into the square edges. The result has three dominant shapes: red attacker, ochre shield, green defender. The weapon and cream impact carry the interaction. At 64 and 48 px the two sides and shield stay readable; at 32 px the precise spear contact is less distinct, but the opposing colors and weapon diagonal survive. The 32 px thumbnail is a stress check, not proof that every detail reads there.

The rounded and Android circular crops preserve the key action. The defender's headwrap ties and lower garments can crop; they are secondary. The shield remains intentionally large. It helps recognition, though it could still dominate for a viewer who does not immediately notice the spear.

## Remaining limits

- The design communicates historical combat more directly than province management, card drafting or kingdom building. Packing those systems into the same icon would restore the rejected complexity.
- The Vietnamese connection comes primarily from the approved Đông Hồ drawing language and the game's surrounding name/context. Anonymous clothing and a shield alone cannot uniquely identify Vietnamese history.
- It is a compact combat vignette, not a literal representation of an entire formation or an authenticated historical scene.
- The user has not yet confirmed that they like this subject. Technical mobile readiness and our design recommendation do not establish that preference.
- No physical-device preview or native build was run during this pass.
