# Vietnamese hero wardrobe, second drawing pass

Reviewed 6 September 2026. This pass corrects a production failure: v1 preserved the old geometric silhouettes so faithfully that headbands looked like bars and robes like rounded blocks. A PNG conversion is not a clothing redesign.

## Evidence and its limits

- [Vietnam National Museum of History, Nguyễn garments in the collection, Đinh Quỳnh Hoa, Collections Management, 2015](https://baotanglichsu.vn/vi/Articles/3096/18741/y-phuc-thoi-nguyen-tai-bao-tang-lich-su-quoc-gia.html). Read the catalogue descriptions and visually inspected the article's red rectangular-collar robe and several round-collar robes. Observable construction: broad sleeves, continuous fabric panels, a neck opening, contrasting facings and separate chest insignia. These are Nguyễn objects, not references for every dynasty. The collection includes worship garments; they must not be presented indiscriminately as garments worn by historical rulers.
- [Trần Quang Đức, *Ngàn năm áo mũ*, 2013, digital scan](https://dilib.vn/img/pdf/10952-ngan-nam-ao-mu-thuviensach.vn.pdf). Additional targeted reading: PDF navigation pages 43–44, 69–70, 95–96, 187 and 243. Early armour has textual evidence, but its exact appearance is uncertain. Lý–Trần guardian sculpture provides qualified comparison, not a surviving battlefield uniform. Đinh military caps have a recorded flat top, four joined sides and narrower upper portion. Later military clothing is period-specific; Nguyễn soldiers should not automatically inherit medieval armour. PDF image extraction failed, so the book illustrations were not visually inspected.
- [Nguyễn dress regulations exhibition, National Museum / National Archives](https://vnmh.com.vn/vi/Articles/3091/73223/che-djo-y-quan-trieu-nguyen.html). Rank, occasion, civil and military roles differ. A game rarity tier is not a documented court grade.
- [Hanoi Museum, northern women's clothing in early twentieth-century photographs](https://baotanghanoi.com.vn/en/phu-nu-ha-noi-trong-trang-phuc-truyen-thong-dau-the-ki-20-ve-dep-cua-su-giao-thoi/). Previously inspected photographs guide late northern wrap, front-panel and broad-hat construction. They do not establish medieval use.
- [Museum study of Đông Hồ prints](https://baotanglichsu.vn/VI/Articles/3096/19432/tim-hieu-ve-dong-tranh-dan-gian-djong-ho.html) and [UNESCO craft description](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/craft-of-making-ong-h-folk-woodblock-printings-01737?USL=01737). Separate pigment shapes and expressive black contours inform the drawing treatment. Sparse fold strokes should explain cloth, rather than simulate photographic gloss.

The broader reference review remains in `vietnamese-portrait-research-findings.md`. Neither a Vietnamese name on an old asset nor a modern costume film authenticates its silhouette. Shared historical court forms require evidence of Vietnamese use; Chinese costume illustrations are not substituted as Vietnamese references.

## Production decisions

1. Redraw all 140 wardrobe, headwear and jewellery resources. Retain v1 face anatomy and hairstyles. Preserve both older art packs.
2. Draw primary garments as coherent bust assemblies with their own neck opening, shoulder slope, continuous lapels and drape. The v1 layering of unrelated strips creates broken collars; v2 selects one primary garment and avoids stacking incompatible duplicate facings over it. Auxiliary pieces still have individual PNG resources.
3. Cloth bands need folded edges, a tied junction and naturally falling tails. Avoid rigid rectangular bars, giant bows and unsupported royal jewels. A simple practical band is an artistic reconstruction, not evidence of a universal Tây Sơn red-headband uniform.
4. Preserve important distinctions: open wound wraps versus closed headcloths; a mỏ-quạ point versus a standing cap; conical nón versus broad flat northern hats; round collar versus right-crossing lapels versus the late standing collar; rectangular Nhật Bình facings versus a generic V neck.
5. Armour variants are restrained interpretations. Lacing, overlapping plates, edge binding and cloth beneath should convey construction. No invented fantasy horns, shining plate cuirasses or arbitrary shoulder monsters. Unsupported crown IDs remain excluded from normal Đông Hồ portrait/creator presentation, as in v1; generated compatibility resources do not authenticate them.
6. Use near-white pigment for runtime-tinted textiles, warm-black contours and sparse grey fold blocks. Fixed-colour headwear and accessories use indigo, charcoal, earth brown, cinnabar, ochre and restrained jade green. No cinematic lighting, metallic gradients or heavy noise.

## Historical corrections to enforce in the new presentation

- Do not use medieval armour as the default Nguyễn general's dress.
- Do not describe the 1744 Đàng Trong reform as an instant nationwide replacement.
- Do not call round collars a Lê invention.
- Do not infer exact historical rank from game rarity, or use a dragon badge as a generic civil bird badge.
- Do not present a film-derived Đinh helmet or fan-scale armour as archaeological evidence.

This is historically informed game illustration, not a claim that every generated variant reconstructs a documented individual object.
