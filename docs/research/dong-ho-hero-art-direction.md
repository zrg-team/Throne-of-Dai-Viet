# Đông Hồ hero portraits: source review and acceptance rules

Reviewed 6 September 2026, after the user required Vietnamese references before further generation.

## References actually read

- [UNESCO: Craft of making Đông Hồ Folk woodblock printings](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/craft-of-making-ong-h-folk-woodblock-printings-01737?USL=01737). Hand-carved blocks, natural pigments, shell-coated paper, and black contours printed after the colors. This establishes the print medium, not historical dress.
- [Vietnam National Museum of History: Ngàn năm áo mũ](https://baotanglichsu.vn/vi/Articles/3129/14969/ngan-nam-ao-mu.html). Museum overview of Trần Quang Đức's study of court and commoner clothing, 1009–1945. A book overview does not authenticate every costume in the game.
- Trần Quang Đức, *Ngàn năm áo mũ* (2013): consulted the introductory chronology and sections on Đinh Tự headwear, Trần hair, and Nguyễn Nhật Bình dress. The available digital scan's PDF pages 65–66, 74, and 156 are navigation locations, not printed-page citations. These were targeted text readings, not a claim to have read the entire book or visually inspected its illustrations. Prefer the published edition when checking citations.
- [Liam C. Kelley: A History of Court and Commoner Clothing in Vietnam](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5b34s57s), five-page scholarly review (2016), read in full. It explains that Vietnamese courts selected and adapted dress conventions across time. Shared historical elements require evidence of Vietnamese use; they do not authorize substituting Chinese costumes.
- [Vietnam National Museum of History / National Archives exhibition: Chế độ Y quan triều Nguyễn](https://vnmh.com.vn/vi/Articles/3091/73223/che-djo-y-quan-trieu-nguyen.html). Dress varies by rank and occasion; civil and military Phốc Đầu forms differ. The article documents a collection of records and objects, rather than a universal outfit for Vietnamese people.
- [Hanoi Museum: Women of Hanoi in traditional attire in the early twentieth century](https://baotanghanoi.com.vn/en/phu-nu-ha-noi-trong-trang-phuc-truyen-thong-dau-the-ki-20-ve-dep-cua-su-giao-thoi/). Dated Albert Kahn photographs and descriptions of tứ thân, yếm, ngũ thân, wrapped hair, broad nón, and blackened teeth. Applicable to the documented time and place, not a reference for medieval faces or costume.
- Project documents read: `vietnamese-hero-wardrobe.md`, `../30-dong-ho-visual-language.md`, `../23-the-thirteenth-wardrobe.md`, and the current map art documentation. Project reconstructions remain reconstructions; modern film costumes are not primary evidence.

## Decisions for this art pass

1. Keep Vietnamese period, role, sex, monastic status, and the player's saved look assignments. Do not map many distinct historical hats or hairstyles onto a few generic generated substitutes.
2. Draw in flat pigment shapes with a decisive warm-black outline. Use restrained paper/print texture. Dense gold filigree, metallic modeling, fantasy crowns, and modern salon hair are not shortcuts to quality.
3. Treat existing silhouettes as compatibility references, not authenticated costume evidence. Check each costume against its source before preserving or correcting its construction. Image generation may restyle a reference; its output is never historical evidence.
4. Do not use Qing queues, Chinese drama costumes, generic Hanfu, or an undated “Asian imperial” prompt as Vietnamese references. Documented shared elements must retain their Vietnamese date, form, rank, and occasion.
5. Do not manufacture a racial face template. Use varied human facial structures and expressions; historical appearance claims need portraits, objects, or dated local visual evidence. A later folk depiction of an early hero is a style reference, not a contemporary likeness.
6. Early Đinh wardrobe and much Tây Sơn armor remain uncertain. Do not add unsupported helmet crests, heraldry, jewelry, or costume rules. Keep the existing uncertainty explicit.
7. Nguyễn examples must not be projected backward onto Lý or Trần. Monastic portraits retain their separate clothing and hair rules. Rank alone does not entitle every woman to a crown.

## First draft disposition

The initial text-only hair, headwear, and garment sheets were generated before this review. They are **rejected as production sources**: the silhouettes, added ornament, and modern-looking hair were insufficiently constrained. They were never integrated into the runtime or copied over `public/faces/`.

Any accepted new pack must ship separate transparent PNG parts, a runtime raster atlas, provenance, and independent version selection. The original SVG pack must remain usable.
